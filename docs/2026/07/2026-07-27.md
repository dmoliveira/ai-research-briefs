<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week's strongest thread is the rapid maturation of agent evaluation from toy tasks to operational environments. <em>InferenceBench</em> evaluates agents as AI infrastructure engineers: they deploy an OpenAI-compatible server on an H100 and optimize prefill, decode, and throughput under a two-hour budget. <em>DynamicMCPBench</em> turns live MCP servers into trace-grounded tasks with effect-based scoring, and <em>AppWorld-UL</em> adds realistic user clarification, confirmation, and infeasibility handling to tool-use tasks. Together, they make agent failure more legible: current agents know many recipes, but struggle with exploration, long tool chains, user interaction, and reliable multi-step execution.

The second major theme is cost-aware inference and systems work. <em>JAXBench</em> gives TPU kernel optimization the kind of benchmark surface GPU agent work already has, while <em>SonicSampler</em> and <em>DC-Leap</em> target lower-level generation speed. <em>MiniCache</em> is especially relevant for product systems because it treats small models as interface models for reusable program caches, not merely as cheaper substitutes for large models.

Reliability and safety work is also practical. <em>Robust Critics</em> models multi-turn attacks as sequential intent inference, <em>Incomplete Prompt Jailbreaks</em> explains why sentence-completion behavior can delay refusal, and <em>Marking the Wrong Symptoms</em> warns that watermarking can degrade clinical reasoning in ways aggregate benchmarks miss. <em>VeriSimpl</em> is a useful verification paper for natural-language-to-optimization systems because it asks solvers to generate simplified diagnostic tests, giving the LLM a tractable correctness signal.

For recommender and product teams, <em>PromptPack</em> and <em>Benchmarking the Personalization Capabilities of LLMs</em> are the read-first pair. PromptPack reports a concrete 89% LLM cost reduction for annotation agents in online recommendation, while the personalization benchmark finds a plateau in third-party persuasive personalization even for frontier and deep-research agents. The week's best product lesson is that LLM product quality increasingly depends on system shape: batching, caching, user simulation, verification, memory selection, and effect scoring matter as much as model choice.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Agent benchmarks are becoming operational.</strong></p>
    <p>Fresh benchmarks put agents inside inference servers, MCP servers, user-in-the-loop app environments, and TPU kernel optimization loops rather than static QA tasks.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Evaluation is shifting from answers to effects.</strong></p>
    <p>DynamicMCPBench scores reproduced state changes, AppWorld-UL scores user interaction outcomes, and InferenceBench compares optimization trajectories against simple search baselines.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Small models are becoming system components.</strong></p>
    <p>MiniCache and PromptPack both show small or structured model interfaces reducing cost through extraction, batching, caching, and drafting rather than replacing large-model reasoning outright.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Safety failures remain conversational and contextual.</strong></p>
    <p>Multi-turn attacks, incomplete prompts, deceptive representations, and medical watermark side effects all show that safety evaluation needs trajectory and domain-specific checks.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Product personalization needs harder evidence.</strong></p>
    <p>Generative personalization benchmarks are moving beyond "does this sound tailored?" toward logged receiver action, temporal leakage controls, and field validation.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>RAG is moving toward guarantees and richer structure.</strong></p>
    <p>Hypergraph RAG and conformal citation guards both target retrieval-backed systems where factuality depends on structured extraction, attribution, and calibrated uncertainty.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>The framework generates realistic goals for MCP servers, records successful trajectories, distills them into effect checkpoints, and scores agents on whether they reproduce those effects.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Tool-use benchmarks often score final answers rather than whether an agent caused the intended external state changes.</p></div>

    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is a likely template for internal enterprise agent evaluation because it lets teams test their own servers without inventing a bespoke benchmark stack.</p></div>
