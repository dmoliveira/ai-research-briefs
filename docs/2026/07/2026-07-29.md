<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week's most important item is <em>Kimi K3</em>, a 2.8T-parameter open-weight MoE model with a 1-million-token context window, native vision, and explicit post-training for coding, agents, and long-horizon execution. The headline is not merely scale; the paper claims infrastructure and algorithmic changes around Delta Attention, Stable LatentMoE, expert-parallel training, million-token agentic RL, and deployment. If the release holds up, it is a meaningful push toward open frontier intelligence, though the evidence should be treated as vendor-reported until third-party evaluations land.

The agent-systems cluster is also strong. <em>The Physics of Multi-Turn Long-Horizon Planning</em> studies planning across pretraining, GRPO, on-policy distillation, and multi-teacher integration in a controlled environment. <em>DataOrchestra</em> turns pretraining data processing into per-example orchestration, which is directly relevant to teams trying to improve smaller models without blindly applying expensive cleaning pipelines. <em>Efficiency Matters in Autonomous Research</em> argues that autonomous research systems should be judged by search efficiency, not only final best result, and proposes a fluid-search portfolio method.

The systems papers are the most immediately actionable for AI engineering. <em>Denial of Deadline</em> identifies a new failure mode for distributed inference pipelines: shaped workload bursts can delay the higher-quality slow path past application deadlines, causing accuracy collapse without touching weights or data. <em>LOCKS</em>, <em>PIVOT</em>, and <em>DraftExpert</em> all attack inference cost at different points: KV-cache selection, sparse-attention indexing, and expert-offloaded MoE decoding. Together they reinforce a broader trend: production LLM quality is increasingly bounded by runtime design, not just model checkpoints.

For safety and reliability, <em>Agentic Permissions Policy Algebra</em> is the read-first paper because it addresses prompt injection and mixed-confidentiality tool use with a formal information-flow framework. <em>Looping Is Not Reliability</em> is a useful corrective to naive coding-agent loops: repeated revision can lose a correct patch unless verifier evidence is bound to exact program state. <em>D-Score</em> and <em>What do Reward Models Memorize?</em> add lightweight reliability signals for hallucination and preference-model failure modes.

For product and recommender teams, the recommender trio is unusually relevant. <em>UniR^2</em> reports an industrial decoder-only architecture that unifies generative recall and multi-objective ranking, <em>LaRec</em> explores latent reasoning for efficient LLM-based recommendation, and <em>IIMRec</em> shows how one high-quality item-item graph can improve multiple stages of multimodal recommendation. The practical lesson is consistent with the rest of the week: systems that reuse structure across stages are becoming more important than isolated model improvements.
