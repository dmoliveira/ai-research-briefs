<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

The highest-signal item this week is Google's <em>ScientistOne</em> / Science One Framework work. It addresses a structural weakness in autonomous research systems: generated papers can look coherent while containing fabricated references, unreproducible scores, or method descriptions that do not match code. The proposed Chain-of-Evidence architecture and audit protocol make evidence provenance a first-class constraint. That matters beyond AI scientists: product teams building long-running agents need claim-level evidence trails, not just good-looking final reports.

Computer-use and coding-agent evaluation also moved forward. <em>OSReward</em> targets scalable verification of computer-use trajectories, <em>ORCA-bench</em> puts agents in an oncall incident-response setting with logs, traces, metrics, and code, and <em>Change2Task</em> generates executable coding-agent tasks from repository changes. <em>Qwen-UI-Agent</em> is the broad foundation-model entry in this cluster, framing GUI agents around cross-platform workflows, long-horizon operation, and self-improvement.

The security and reliability cluster is unusually concrete. <em>MIND</em> defends memory-augmented agents against memory injection; <em>Piggybacking on Perception</em> studies concurrent audio prompt injections against multimodal agents; <em>Agentic Misalignment in Summer 2026</em> provides controlled case studies of sabotage, fraud assistance, motivated mislabeling, and external-disclosure coaching. <em>PAIChecker</em> is narrower but important because it challenges the quality of SWE-bench-like PR-issue pairings.

On systems, <em>SmartGen</em> is the strongest serving paper because it tackles disaggregated prefill/decode deployments under network limits through selective KV-cache transfer. Retrieval and product systems continue to converge: <em>DualG-MRAG</em> separates macro-reasoning from micro-matching for multimodal RAG, while <em>ROCS</em> and <em>Feedback-Grounded Policy Discovery</em> show recommender work moving toward request-level compute reuse and recommendation-specific feedback policies.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Agent outputs need provenance, not polish.</strong></p>
    <p>ScientistOne, LEDGERMIND-style evidence ledgers, and oncall/coding benchmarks all reward traceability from final answer back to source, tool, code, or state change.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Computer-use agents are getting their evaluation stack.</strong></p>
    <p>Reward models, local inference-time scaling studies, GUI-agent technical reports, and incident-response benchmarks are turning screen agents into measurable systems.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Memory is now a security boundary.</strong></p>
    <p>Persistent agent memory needs denoising, source support, recovery boundaries, and intent checks because poisoned memories can steer future behavior.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Serving bottlenecks are becoming network bottlenecks.</strong></p>
    <p>Disaggregated LLM serving moves KV cache between nodes; SmartGen shows that deciding what to transfer is central to practical self-hosted deployments.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>Recommenders are borrowing language-model policy ideas.</strong></p>
    <p>Generative recommendation work now uses LLM-derived policies, feedback grounding, semantic IDs, and request-level compute sharing.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card">
    <p><strong>RAG is becoming more structured and multimodal.</strong></p>
    <p>Fresh RAG papers separate graph-level reasoning, fine-grained matching, and source provenance instead of treating retrieval as a flat top-k document step.</p>
  </div>
</div>
