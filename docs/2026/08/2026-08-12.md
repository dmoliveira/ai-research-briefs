<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week is dominated by agent infrastructure papers that treat the harness itself as the object of optimization and governance. <em>SHE</em> evolves safety boundaries from trajectories across system prompts, rules, safety memory, and tool policies. <em>ElasticBack</em> shows why that matters: a single installed skill can carry a dormant conditional backdoor that fires only under a paired trigger and rule. <em>BCSD</em> attacks a more benign version of the same skill problem by training agents to actually use external skills rather than merely receive them in context.

Self-improving agents are also getting more concrete. <em>Rethinking Self-Evolving Agents</em> compares open-ended optimizer control with prescribed pipelines and suggests that strong frontier optimizers may compose the improvement process themselves if budgets and evaluation stay external. <em>OpenLoopEvolve</em> makes loop policies versioned, comparable, and rollback-capable, while <em>Tree-of-Experience</em> organizes experience as a calibrated reasoning tree. The most provocative conceptual entry is <em>Agentic Auto-Research is Fuzz Testing</em>, which argues that research agents need dense progress signals and feedback-directed mutation, not just larger generate-and-rank batches.

On model and product systems, <em>Matryoshka Language Model Suites</em> is the most practically relevant efficiency paper: nested 500M, 1.5B, and 3B models match independent baselines while cutting suite training compute and improving speculative decoding throughput. <em>KVDiagnosis</em> gives long-context teams a diagnostic benchmark for KV-cache compression failures instead of relying on aggregate scores. Google Research's AMIE video consultation work is a major multimodal agent demonstration, but its caveats are central: simulated patient actors, prototype infrastructure, and no real-world deployment claim. The recommender items are narrower but useful, especially InforID's adaptive semantic-ID capacity allocation.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Harnesses are becoming learned, audited artifacts.</strong></p><p>Safety prompts, rule banks, memory, tool policies, and loop policies are now treated as versioned surfaces that can be evolved and rolled back.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Skills are a new supply-chain risk.</strong></p><p>The same reusable instruction bundles that make agents modular can carry conditional attacks or create skill-utilization gaps.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Self-evolution needs external measurement.</strong></p><p>The strongest self-improvement papers leave budgets, objectives, data boundaries, and evaluation outside the optimizer even when the optimizer controls the route.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Research automation is being reframed as search.</strong></p><p>The fuzz-testing analogy shifts attention from ranking completed experiments to dense feedback that guides the next intervention.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Efficiency work is getting diagnostic.</strong></p><p>Nested model suites and KV-cache diagnosis both ask where efficiency can be gained without silently changing behavior.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Multimodal agents are entering high-stakes simulations.</strong></p><p>AMIE and GUI-grounding work show stronger perception-action loops, but both require careful caveats about generalization and deployment readiness.</p></div>
</div>

    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Agent skills are installable instruction bundles, but defenses often inspect them statically or look for attacks that fire broadly.</p></div>
