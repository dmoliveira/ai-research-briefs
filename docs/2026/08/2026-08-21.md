<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week's strongest work is about controlling agent action at the system boundary. <em>Bounded Agents</em> reframes prompt-injection risk as a delegation and authorization problem, using an Agentic Principal Chain to carry task scope and session state across tool calls and sub-agent delegation. <em>PACE</em> applies the same pattern to DeFi execution by binding a deterministic policy decision to the exact on-chain transaction bytes. <em>Beyond the Transcript</em> adds a multi-agent twist: harmful coordination may occur through hidden latent channels, so monitors may need to connect private states to public actions rather than inspect only transcripts.

The agent-training and evaluation story is also sharper. <em>SkillGate</em> identifies selector credit starvation as a structural failure in outcome-rewarded skill selection and trains a separate credit channel for the tokens that choose a skill. <em>ComponentBench</em>, accepted at COLM 2026, fills the gap between atomic GUI grounding tests and long workflow benchmarks with 2,910 component-level tasks. <em>Engineering Reliable Coding Agents</em> is a large system-level review arguing that coding-agent failures often originate in harness, state, retrieval, permission, and review layers rather than the model alone.

For memory, retrieval, and evaluator operations, the most practical papers all separate the artifact from the final answer. <em>D2ACCI</em> requires paired evidence and localizable traces before promoting memory changes. <em>FinRCA-Bench</em> shows that financial root-cause accuracy can rise from 2.05% to 72.44% by changing only retrieval architecture, while strict evidence-contract accuracy remains low. <em>Metrics That Write Themselves</em> evolves cheap executable evaluators from counterexamples, and Netflix's <em>Lifecycle of LLM-as-a-Judge</em> describes judge construction, deployment, and drift monitoring in a large recommendation-explanation system.

The recommender-system signal is unusually product-shaped. <em>The Lifecycle of LLM-as-a-Judge</em> is valuable because it connects judge alignment to a five-week A/B test over tens of millions of members. <em>Pairwise Ranking Outperforms Single-Action RL</em> argues for offline explanation pools plus lightweight selection instead of per-request LLM generation. <em>FLEXRec</em> improves compact LLM-based sequential recommenders through adaptive layer-wise exits while preserving full-corpus ranking.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Authorization is moving outside the model.</strong></p><p>Bounded Agents and PACE both enforce policies with explicit state, signatures, scopes, or transaction records rather than relying on model self-restraint.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Skill systems need their own learning signal.</strong></p><p>SkillGate and earlier skill-harm work make skill selection look like a policy action that needs attribution, budgets, and regression checks.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Agent benchmarks are becoming diagnostic.</strong></p><p>ComponentBench, FinRCA-Bench, and code-review protocols score components, evidence, and process, not just final task success.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Evaluation artifacts are becoming production assets.</strong></p><p>Netflix's judge lifecycle and EvalCEGAR-style executable metrics both treat evaluators as systems that require construction, monitoring, and maintenance.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Memory improvements need trace-localizable evidence.</strong></p><p>D2ACCI argues that aggregate memory scores hide whether ingestion, retrieval, filtering, or generation actually improved.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Scientific agents still need external validation.</strong></p><p>Eureka and Anthropic's biology/chemistry work are promising, but the claims depend heavily on task design, verification artifacts, and experimental follow-up.</p></div>
</div>

    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15888">https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15888</a></span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Tags</span><span>agent security, delegation, authorization, prompt injection, multi-agent systems</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Reading profile</span><span>35-50 min - Relevance 5/5 - Quality 5/5</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>The paper introduces the Agentic Principal Chain, which evaluates each request against accumulated session state and delegated scope rather than treating tool calls as independent authorization events.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Prompt injection becomes dangerous when an agent has authority to act; static permissions fail when individually allowed actions combine into prohibited outcomes.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Production agents increasingly act across cloud services, tools, and sub-agents, making authority propagation a core systems concern.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>APC carries delegated scope and budgets forward, applies six authorization checks, uses composition closure over prior actions, and enforces decisions outside the model.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>Across 3,154 instances, AgentDojo exfiltration falls from 75-100% to 0% in four domains; APC blocks all 544 InjecAgent data-stealing cases; p99 authorization latency is 0.24 ms.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Agent safety needs stateful, task-scoped authorization that survives delegation and checks combinations of effects.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is one of the clearest recent arguments that agent security is a runtime architecture problem, not just a model-alignment or prompt-hardening problem.</p></div>
  </div>

  <p class="entry-summary">A transaction-level framework that cryptographically binds verifier approval to the exact transaction submitted by an LLM-based DeFi agent.</p>
  <div class="entry-meta-grid">
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Published / updated</span><span>2026-08-18 / 2026-08-18; arXiv ID 2608.17220</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Authors</span><span>Rabimba Karanjai, Yang Lu, Richard Williamson, Hemanth Hm, Prakhar Mehrotra, Lei Xu, Weidong Shi</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Affiliations</span><span>Not listed in arXiv metadata</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17220">https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17220</a></span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Tags</span><span>agent security, DeFi, policy verification, smart accounts, prompt injection</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Reading profile</span><span>35-50 min - Relevance 5/5 - Quality 5/5</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>PACE inserts typed transaction intents, a deterministic policy verifier, and signed Policy Decision Records between an LLM agent and on-chain execution.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>An LLM planner can approve one thing and submit another, especially under prompt injection or transaction-byte manipulation.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>DeFi is a useful stress case because mistakes are high consequence, executable, and hard to reverse.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The system binds approved intent, policy, simulation report, and execution bytes with replay and expiration protection, then enforces signatures in a Solidity smart account.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>On 40 tasks and 2,800 deterministic trials, PACE reports 0.00 unsafe execution and 0.00 benign false-positive rate, while the unguarded baseline has 0.80 unsafe execution; gas overhead is 29,826-31,822.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>For high-consequence actions, safe agent execution requires policy attestations tied to concrete effects, not only natural-language approval.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>The DeFi domain is narrow, but the design pattern generalizes: approve structured effects, bind approval to the exact payload, and keep enforcement outside the model.</p></div>
  </div>

  <h3>SkillGate: Training In-Policy Skill Selection in Long-Horizon Agents</h3>
  <p class="entry-summary">A training method that gives skill-selection tokens their own action-local credit instead of burying them in sequence-level outcome reward.</p>
  <div class="entry-meta-grid">
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Published / updated</span><span>2026-08-19 / 2026-08-19; arXiv ID 2608.18852</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Authors</span><span>Qingyao Li, Wenxiang Jiao, Shuai Shao, Kangning Zhang, Yuan Lu, Yi Guo, Weiwen Liu, Weinan Zhang, Yong Yu</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Affiliations</span><span>Not listed in arXiv metadata</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18852">https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18852</a></span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Tags</span><span>agent skills, reinforcement learning, credit assignment, long-horizon agents, policy training</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Reading profile</span><span>35-50 min - Relevance 5/5 - Quality 5/5</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>SkillGate treats the decision to read a skill as an in-episode policy action and assigns a separate local advantage to the skill-naming tokens.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Outcome-rewarded RL gives almost no useful signal to the few tokens that choose a skill, and the signal can become wrong-signed when execution fails after a correct skill choice.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Agent platforms increasingly expose large skill libraries, so selecting procedural context is becoming a major runtime decision.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The method partitions token support into outcome credit for execution and action-local credit for the skill-selection tokens.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>On five agentic benchmarks with a 16-candidate slate, SkillGate lifts a 9B policy from 40.8% to 53.2% trial success, while reading fewer skills and reducing exposure to misleading candidates by two thirds.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Skill selection should be trained as its own decision, not treated as incidental prompt text inside a long trajectory.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is highly relevant to agent platforms: as skill libraries grow, the selector becomes part of the policy and needs attribution as much as the executor does.</p></div>
  </div>
</article>
