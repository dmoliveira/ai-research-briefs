<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week's strongest thread is that agent safety no longer composes automatically across turns, tools, or harness layers. <em>Safety Does Not Compose</em> argues that trajectory-scoped monitors miss attacks whose evidence is spread across unattended iterations. <em>When Context Gets Root</em> names a related harness problem: low-privilege content can be promoted into higher-privilege context during agent execution. <em>PLCBench</em> then shows why these failures matter beyond text, measuring whether autonomous LLM agents can turn PLC access into sustained physical process impact.

The red-team and guardrail papers sharpen both sides of safety evaluation. <em>NeuronFuzz</em> uses internal safety-neuron activations as cheap continuous feedback for jailbreak discovery. <em>RedEvoAgent</em> evolves reusable attack skills from prior trajectories. <em>The Guard That Cried Wolf</em> reminds teams that guardrail false positives are also deployment failures when authorized actions are rejected because of surface-level scary names.

The agent benchmark signal is becoming more operational. <em>INTENT-AS-A-TOOL</em> converts latent intent into a tool-call probability that can be tracked online. <em>BTS-AgentBench</em> turns read-only industrial telemetry into replayable, evidence-attributed operator episodes. These are useful patterns for product teams that need reproducible agent evaluation rather than anecdotal traces.

On the model-systems side, <em>TTPO</em> and <em>CritICL</em> both improve reasoning at inference or test time without ordinary labeled training. Retrieval work is more skeptical: evidence-aware retrieval and confidence-gated retrieval improve some operating points but do not automatically predict downstream answer quality. Recommender and deployment papers focus on temporal video preference structure, order-consistent scoring, pruned-model degeneration, and task-conditioned MoE activation.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Agent safety is moving outside the single call.</strong></p><p>Loop-level state, harness context construction, and tool-side effects all need evaluation across iterations and execution boundaries.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Security evaluation is becoming cheaper and more adaptive.</strong></p><p>Neuron-guided fuzzing and skill-evolving red teams reduce reliance on expensive full-response sampling and fixed jailbreak templates.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Guardrails need false-positive benchmarks.</strong></p><p>Authorized actions that look dangerous can be blocked for irrelevant lexical reasons, so deployment safety now includes over-refusal analysis.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Replayability is becoming the evaluation baseline.</strong></p><p>Matched trajectory replay and telemetry-to-episode pipelines make it easier to separate real improvement from changed sampling conditions.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Retrieval metrics are being re-grounded in use.</strong></p><p>Evidence-aware and confidence-gated retrieval papers show that better-looking retrieval signals must be tied to the actual downstream decision they support.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Efficiency papers are more behavior-specific.</strong></p><p>New work targets test-time policy updates, failure-mode guidance, repetition after pruning, activation outliers, and task-conditioned MoE routing.</p></div>
</div>
