<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week is about whether AI engineering teams can trust the instruments around their models. <em>Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement</em> is the sharpest warning: black-box LLM judges on shared endpoints can look operationally clean while failing basic repeatability gates. <em>Interface-Induced Trajectory Censoring</em> makes the same point for tool use: a model can emit valid calls that disappear at the adapter boundary, changing benchmark scores without changing weights.

Coding-agent evaluation also matured. <em>SWE-Gate</em> argues that passing tests misses real review constraints, <em>PatchBench</em> probes whether vulnerability patches generalize beyond a proof-of-concept crash, and <em>When Models Edit Too Much</em> measures whether repairs are reviewable and minimal. <em>Terminal-Universe</em> is especially practical because it turns past terminal trajectories into reusable executable environments for training and evaluation.

The model-training papers are more mixed but still useful. <em>Compile by Training</em> turns natural-language specifications into local neural functions. <em>Headroom-Drift Replay</em> isolates replay selection for GRPO-style post-training, and <em>Free Pause Tokens</em> proposes parallel thinking streams that add compute without adding sequence length. <em>Knowledge Acquisition During Pre-training?</em> gives controlled evidence that auxiliary views can outperform simple repetition under fixed token budgets.

Retrieval and recommender work remains product-facing. <em>Spruce</em> is the standout systems paper, co-designing compact binary embeddings with private outsourced retrieval. <em>RuleMem</em> induces reusable rules for long-term conversational memory. <em>CLEAR</em> shifts conversational retrieval from topical similarity to answerability, and <em>EPIC</em> adds item-level competition to semantic-ID diffusion recommendation.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Evaluation is failing at the interface, not just the model.</strong></p><p>Endpoint drift, parser-template interactions, and missing review constraints can dominate reported performance.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Coding-agent benchmarks are moving beyond pass/fail tests.</strong></p><p>Patch similarity, review constraints, vulnerability generalization, and edit minimality are becoming first-class metrics.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Trajectory artifacts are becoming reusable infrastructure.</strong></p><p>Logs, terminal histories, agent decisions, and interaction traces are increasingly treated as sources for new environments, memories, and eval gates.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Chain-of-thought legibility is losing its status as a safety proxy.</strong></p><p>Several papers distinguish readable traces from causal importance, deception mechanisms, and reliable process supervision.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Post-training efficiency is targeting interaction cost.</strong></p><p>Replay selection, test generation, and compact local functions all reduce repeated calls to expensive models or environments.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Retrieval is becoming more structured and privacy-aware.</strong></p><p>Answerability, rules, private embeddings, semantic IDs, and teacher reasoning distillation all reshape what gets retrieved or recommended.</p></div>
</div>

  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>The paper audits whether repeated black-box judge calls to the same named model behave like a stable measurement instrument.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Leaderboards, training-data filters, and automated release gates often assume that model-as-judge scores are repeatable across time and serving infrastructure.</p></div>

    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>On shared endpoints, a model name is not enough to define a frozen measurement instrument.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is a must-read for eval owners: before arguing about benchmark validity, measure whether the instrument can reproduce itself.</p></div>
