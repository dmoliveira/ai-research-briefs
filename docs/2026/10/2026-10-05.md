<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week is about agent systems becoming harder to judge by single-pass accuracy. <em>Sharpening Tax in Post-Training</em> argues that RL-tuned agents often become more consistent but less diverse under repeated sampling, which matters for teams that use large rollout budgets. <em>AnyAct</em> and <em>FloWright</em> push in a complementary direction: agent systems need self-evolving action spaces and workflow-level training signals, not just better base models. <em>Federated Agent Optimization</em> widens the frame by treating agent memory, tools, skills, rewards, and structured knowledge as private experience that may be shared only through controlled abstractions.

Safety and evaluation work is unusually concrete. <em>FIGS</em> separates truthfulness from empathy in multi-turn sycophancy tests, while <em>AI Agents are Vulnerable to Radicalization</em> shows how multi-agent influence can amplify pre-existing beliefs. Anthropic's GLM-5.3 analysis is less a benchmark paper than an operational warning: open or weakly safeguarded cyber-capable models can change the threat surface for both attackers and defenders.

Retrieval and infrastructure entries are practical. A controlled RAG comparison finds that reranking and late interaction drive scientific QA quality more reliably than more agentic retrieval variants. Google's TEE-based federated learning system is the most production-grounded privacy item, because it combines externally verifiable server-side computation, transparency logs, reproducible builds, and a deployed Gboard use case. The attention survey is worth reading because it reframes long-context efficiency as contextual-memory lifecycle design rather than just cheaper attention.

Recommendation systems get four strong production-facing items: reasoning generative recommendation from Kuaishou, attack propagation in agentic recommenders, conditional value of LLM-generated user profiles, and fleet-scale effective training time optimization. The common thread is that recommenders are becoming foundation-model and agent-adjacent systems, but their real constraints remain reliability, coverage, latency, poisoning resistance, and fleet utilization.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Post-training has an exploration cost.</strong></p><p>Agent RL can improve pass@1 while compressing solution diversity, so rollout-budget scalability needs to be measured directly.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Action spaces are becoming managed infrastructure.</strong></p><p>Tool ecosystems now need reliability pruning, hierarchical retrieval, heterogeneous observations, and cost-aware orchestration.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Workflow agents need credit assignment.</strong></p><p>Training only the workflow generator underuses the failure signal produced by specialized roles that execute the workflow.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Safety tests are moving into sustained interaction.</strong></p><p>Sycophancy, radicalization, and cyber misuse are being evaluated over trajectories rather than isolated prompts.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Privacy systems are becoming auditable compute platforms.</strong></p><p>TEEs, transparency logs, reproducible builds, and access policies are turning privacy from a claim into inspectable infrastructure.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Recommendation systems are absorbing LLM patterns selectively.</strong></p><p>LLM summaries, semantic IDs, reasoning traces, and agent reflection help in some regimes but create new attack and coverage risks.</p></div>
</div>

  <h3>Sharpening Tax in Post-Training</h3>
  <p class="entry-summary">A diagnostic for when RL post-training improves single-shot success but reduces an agent's solution coverage under repeated sampling.</p>
  <div class="entry-meta-grid">
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Published / updated</span><span>2026-10-01 / 2026-10-01; arXiv ID 2610.01509</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Authors</span><span>Changdae Oh, Qi Zeng, Qi Qi, Andrey Zhmoginov, Deren Lei, Yun He, Hoang Phan, Hangoo Kang, Azalia Mirhoseini, Sharon Li</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Affiliations</span><span>Not listed in arXiv listing metadata</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509">https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509</a></span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Tags</span><span>post-training, RL, agents, pass@K, test-time compute</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Reading profile</span><span>35-50 min - Relevance 5/5 - Quality 5/5</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>The paper shows that post-trained agents can become sharper and more reliable at pass@1 while losing coverage when many attempts are allowed.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Agent evaluations often reward single-shot accuracy, missing whether training narrows the model away from alternative valid strategies.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Production coding, tool-use, and browser agents commonly rely on retries, ensembles, or parallel rollouts rather than one attempt.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The authors compare 14 base/post-trained model pairs across three agentic benchmarks and propose Sharpening Tax plus posterior-tempered group sampling.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>The tax appears across most tested settings and correlates with reduced repeated-sampling scalability; PTGS reduces the tax in two RL environments.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Post-training should be evaluated against both reliability and exploration coverage, especially when inference budgets are large.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is a must-read for agent builders because it explains why a model that feels more polished can still be worse for search-heavy workflows.</p></div>
  </div>
</article>

  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>AnyAct unifies primitive GUI actions and semantic APIs into a self-evolving action space with hierarchical retrieval and reliability-aware orchestration.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Agents face too many tools for context windows, unstable tool quality, and feedback split across pixels, text, and structured observations.</p></div>

  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>FloWright optimizes one or more workflow roles using a hierarchical reward signal derived from the workflow harness itself.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Workflow training often updates the generator while the specialized agents that cause successes or failures remain fixed.</p></div>

    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is useful because it points away from hand-tuning agent prompts and toward training the whole choreography of the workflow.</p></div>
