<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week is unusually strong for agent infrastructure. <em>CLIFT</em> turns expensive judge feedback into conformal self-verification signals for web-agent training and judge-free test-time trajectory selection. <em>MemPilot</em> treats agent memory as a runtime policy over cost, latency, visual access, evidence amount, and model choice instead of a static preprocessing step. <em>HERA</em> makes abstention a harness-environment co-evolution problem, which is the right framing for infeasible tasks that change shape over time.

Search and evidence work continues to move beyond query reformulation. <em>T-Search</em> releases an open-weight agentic retriever that returns evidence chunks and justifications for hard multi-step search. <em>Programmatic Search Agents</em> argues that the unit of search should be executable candidate processing, not just another query string. <em>Wikidata Search Traces</em> provides persistent-state graph-search trajectories for training agents that need to answer from structured knowledge rather than memorized popularity.

Behavioral safety and privacy work is concrete. <em>Base Models Can Reason By Taking a Cue From Training Data</em> shows that response-start cues can recover much of the apparent reasoning gain of RL-tuned models and can also steer refusal behavior. <em>Before Agent Tells The Lie</em> suggests deception can be detected in hidden states before it appears in actions. <em>TranScope</em> is a hardware-side-channel warning: training-data membership may leak through microarchitectural footprints even in constant-time inference.

Systems and recommender entries are practical. <em>OVAL</em> improves KV-cache page retrieval by accounting for output error, <em>LoGRA</em> cuts memory for LLM RL post-training with low-rank gradient sketches, and <em>Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II</em> exploits fixed-point behavior to make recurrent language models cheaper to train, decode, and update. <em>SPRIG</em> and <em>MatrixFormer</em> show two different routes for recommender and matrix-completion foundation models: structured semantic IDs plus KG paths, and synthetic pretraining over matrix-native structure.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Agent verification is becoming reusable.</strong></p><p>Self-verifier banks, conformal certification, and trajectory selection are being designed as portable assets rather than per-task judge calls.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Memory is moving to runtime policy.</strong></p><p>Agent systems increasingly decide when to curate, retrieve, inspect raw history, or spend visual/model compute based on the current query.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Search agents need workspaces.</strong></p><p>Persistent candidates, executable filtering, and selective evidence presentation are replacing one-shot retrieval dumps.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Safety evaluation is becoming trajectory-aware.</strong></p><p>Deception, compliance, delegation risk, and abstention are being measured across decisions and simulated environments, not isolated prompts.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Long-context cost is now a retrieval problem.</strong></p><p>KV-cache compression and page selection are converging with retrieval methods, but the relevant objective is output fidelity, not just key similarity.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Recommendation is borrowing foundation-model machinery carefully.</strong></p><p>Semantic IDs, KG paths, and matrix-native pretraining can help, but the value is parameter sharing, grounding, and catalog generalization, not generic LLM wrapping.</p></div>
</div>

    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Affiliations</span><span>Not listed in arXiv metadata</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06829">https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06829</a></span></div>

  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>CLIFT trains web agents with certified self-verification questions and reuses the frozen verifier bank for conservative test-time trajectory selection.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Binary task success is too sparse for web-agent RL, while frontier-model judges are costly and unavailable in many deployment settings.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Browser agents increasingly need dense feedback, trace critique, and retry selection without sending every step to an external judge.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The method certifies natural-language verification questions whose URL-conditional evidence agrees with a judge, weights them by polarity-aware lift, and uses them for rewards and trajectory selection.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>The paper reports state-of-the-art open-source performance on WebArena Infinity, transfer to GPT-5.5 on VisualWebArena, and zero-shot gains on Online Mind2Web.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Reusable self-verification can bridge training-time supervision and deployment-time search without persistent judge calls.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is exactly the direction web agents need: durable verifier assets that make rollouts cheaper, more inspectable, and less tied to closed evaluator models.</p></div>

  <h3>HERA: Harness-Environment Co-Evolution for Reliable Agentic Abstention</h3>
  <p class="entry-summary">A framework that evolves both agent harnesses and infeasible task environments to improve abstention without hurting feasible-task completion.</p>
  <div class="entry-meta-grid">
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Published / updated</span><span>2026-10-05 / 2026-10-05; arXiv ID 2610.06563</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Authors</span><span>Han Luo, Bingbing Wen, Guang Yang, Zora Zhiruo Wang, Pan Lu, Lucy Lu Wang</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Affiliations</span><span>Not listed in arXiv metadata</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06563">https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06563</a></span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Tags</span><span>agentic abstention, harness design, reliability, infeasible tasks</span></div>

  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>HERA generates paired feasible and infeasible tasks through environment mutations, then adapts the harness and task generator around previous failures.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Agents often keep acting when a task has no valid solution, and fixed abstention benchmarks underrepresent new failure modes.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Real tool-use environments regularly contain missing files, impossible constraints, revoked permissions, and stale assumptions.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The system mutates solvable tasks into verifiable abstention cases and co-evolves harness behavior against held-out generated environments.</p></div>

    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Abstention reliability can be improved by evolving the execution harness, not only by changing the base model.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is useful because it treats "know when to stop" as an environment-generalization problem, which matches how agents actually fail in products.</p></div>
