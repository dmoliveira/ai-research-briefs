<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

The week is led by concrete agent-infrastructure work. OpenAI's Ironclad collaboration is one of the clearest public examples of converting a complex professional software workflow into trainable and evaluable agent tasks. Google Research's contextual privacy and security report is more conceptual, but it names the right system layer: agents need dynamic policy engines and sandboxing that can reason about context, delegation, and appropriateness, not just static permission prompts.

Workspace and evidence handling are also improving. <em>RunningTab</em> externalizes task obligations, read excerpts, and unopened candidates into an environment-side record, directly addressing a common failure mode in document and repository agents. <em>RECAST</em> treats evidence construction as a learned routing problem over retrieval, filtering, aggregation, and code execution, which is a more realistic shape for analytical tasks than pure similarity search.

Research-agent and post-training work is sharper than usual. OpenAI's mathematics release is scientifically messy but important: it shifts part of the AI-for-research discussion from benchmark scores to public manuscripts, revision protocols, and machine-checkable proof artifacts. <em>Before They Can Solve</em> proposes screens for predicting coding-agent post-training payoff from base models, while <em>ExpDis</em> separates exploration from optimization in RLVR so novelty bonuses do not poison the final student policy.

The systems and safety tail is practical. <em>EngramEdit</em> explores editable conditional memory for factual updates, <em>Open-MMUnlearning</em> gives multimodal unlearning a shared evaluation harness, <em>Reasoning-Token Spikes</em> tests a low-bandwidth deception-related signal, and <em>ResidualQuant</em>, <em>EDR speculative drafting</em>, and corrected two-level softmax sampling target the mundane but decisive bottlenecks in serving and large-catalog learning.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Agent evaluations are becoming domain-native.</strong></p><p>Ironclad-style tasks show that serious agent benchmarks need the target software, business rules, criteria, and failure checks embedded together.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Memory is moving outside the model context.</strong></p><p>RunningTab, Ledger, and contextual policy work all point toward durable environment-side state that agents can inspect, update, and be checked against.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>RAG is becoming evidence computation.</strong></p><p>RECAST and retrieval-instruction analysis both argue that the hard part is not only finding passages, but deriving, filtering, and presenting the right context.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>AI-for-research needs institutions, not only better models.</strong></p><p>The mathematics release, research-agent society proposal, and kernel autoresearch paper all raise governance and validation questions around automated discovery.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Post-training is getting pre-flight diagnostics.</strong></p><p>Base-checkpoint screens for coding agents and exploration-distillation for RLVR both focus on reducing wasted expensive training runs.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Serving efficiency is still a research frontier.</strong></p><p>KV-cache residual quantization, expected-round speculative decoding, and biased-sampling corrections target bottlenecks that directly affect production cost.</p></div>

  <h3>RECAST: Learning to Compute the Right Context through Adaptive Evidence Routing</h3>
  <p class="entry-summary">A learned framework that routes across retrieval, computation, synthesized tools, and answering to construct evidence rather than merely retrieve it.</p>
  <div class="entry-meta-grid">
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Published / updated</span><span>2026-10-07 / 2026-10-07; arXiv ID 2610.10507</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Authors</span><span>Yilun Hao, Krishna Sayana, Isabella Ye, James S Ren, Sukhdeep Sodhi, Craig Boutilier, Chuchu Fan</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Affiliations</span><span>Not listed in arXiv metadata</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Link</span><span><a class="entry-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10507">https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10507</a></span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Tags</span><span>RAG, evidence routing, tool use, GRPO, retrieval</span></div>
    <div class="entry-meta-item"><span class="entry-meta-label">Reading profile</span><span>40-55 min - Relevance 5/5 - Quality 5/5</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-section-grid">
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Summary</span>RECAST uses a lightweight RouterLM to select retrieval and computation operations, a CompilerLM to translate custom operations into code, and an AnswerLM to produce the final solution.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Many grounded tasks require evidence derived by filtering, aggregating, or computing across sources, not evidence that appears directly in a single retrieved chunk.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Agentic RAG systems often remain retrieval-centric even when the real task is analytical data transformation over heterogeneous sources.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The RouterLM is trained with supervised fine-tuning followed by GRPO, choosing primitive or synthesized operations until evidence is sufficient.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>Across six benchmark families, RECAST reports a 75.6% mean success rate, 15.9 points above the strongest large-model baseline, and 15.0-point average gains on three held-out benchmarks.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Evidence construction should be modeled as sequential decision-making over retrieval and computation, not as a static top-k context selection step.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">My take</span>This is one of the week's strongest AI engineering papers because it matches how real analytical assistants work: gather, compute, check, then answer.</p></div>

    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Problem investigated</span>Parallel and semi-autoregressive draft models are usually trained with block-local objectives that ignore how accepted tokens shift future decoding-round starts.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Context</span>Speculative decoding is a major serving optimization, but draft training quality determines whether theoretical speedups become realized latency improvements.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Solutions explored</span>The EDR objective exactly equals expected decoding rounds and enables unbiased stochastic optimization from target-model rollouts plus offline round-count evaluation.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Key results</span>Fine-tuning DSpark and DFly with EDR improves mean accepted length and outperforms existing training objectives across nine math, code, and chat benchmarks.</p></div>
    <div class="entry-section"><p><span class="entry-section-title">Conclusion</span>Draft models should be trained against the global serving objective they are meant to optimize.</p></div>
