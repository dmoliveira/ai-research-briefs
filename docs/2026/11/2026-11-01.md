<h2 id="executive-summary">Executive Summary</h2>

This week's best work is about making agentic systems accountable at the interfaces where ordinary language is weakest. <em>VeriCUA</em> turns computer-use verification into evidence-gathering exploration; <em>PCM</em> makes inter-agent assertions checkable against append-only records; and <em>EnvOS</em> certifies that a changing task environment and its grader still mean what the benchmark says they mean. Together, these papers are a useful answer to the recurrent problem of treating an agent trace as proof.

Data and retrieval work is equally practical. <em>AgenticDS</em> constrains dataset-search agents with typed, deterministic operators and provenance checks, while <em>chATLAS</em> is a deployed example of hybrid, source-attributed RAG for a large scientific collaboration. <em>DeFT-RAG</em> and <em>DuQUAD</em> target the two expensive failure modes in multi-hop RAG: repeated subproblems and noisy evidence buried in long contexts.

On the model side, Google presents continual learning as alternating active learning with a "sleep" consolidation phase. OpenAI's new misalignment-monitoring note and GPT-6.1 Terra system-card addendum make clear that deployment assurance is increasingly a layered operational system, not a single pre-release score. <em>CopyLoop</em> and <em>LRMoE</em> round out the issue with concrete efficiency work for inference and large-model training.

<h2 id="key-trends">Key Trends</h2>

<div class="brief-trend-grid">
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Verification is becoming an agent job.</strong></p><p>Evidence gathering, not a one-shot judge prompt, is the emerging pattern for computer-use completion checks.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Claims need provenance.</strong></p><p>Multi-agent systems are beginning to distinguish authenticated transport from evidence that an assertion or authority is genuine.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Typed control surfaces constrain LLM autonomy.</strong></p><p>Data search and other high-impact workflows increasingly compile model choices into inspectable objects before execution.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>RAG is being optimized for reuse and evidence quality.</strong></p><p>Work this week reduces repeated subqueries, positional bias, and unsupported retrieval choices.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Continual learning needs deliberate consolidation.</strong></p><p>Training systems are exploring off-task phases that protect old knowledge instead of relying on continual gradient updates alone.</p></div>
  <div class="brief-trend-card"><p><strong>Efficiency remains architecture-aware.</strong></p><p>Decoding schedules and MoE resource allocation are now optimized around the structure of recurrent depth, memory, and communication.</p></div>
</div>
